The 2005 version of the invitation-only Linux Kernel Developers' Summit was held on July 18 and 19 in Ottawa. The following are LWN editor Jonathan Corbet's notes from the discussion. 

July 18 sessions: 

  * [The processor panel](<https://lwn.net/Articles/144098/>), being a discussion between the kernel developers and processor architects from AMD, IBM, and Intel. 

  * [I/O Buses](<https://lwn.net/Articles/144100/>), and I/O memory management units in particular. 

  * [Virtual memory topics](<https://lwn.net/Articles/144102/>), including fragmentation, response to memory pressure, and scalability. 

  * [ExecShield](<https://lwn.net/Articles/144107/>); Red Hat's security patches which have only partially been merged into the mainline. 

  * [Virtualization](<https://lwn.net/Articles/144109/>), and how the kernel can better support it. 

  * [The virtual filesystem](<https://lwn.net/Articles/144110/>), and various topics related to the VFS. 

July 19 (Tuesday) sessions: [![\[Linus Torvalds\]](https://static.lwn.net/images/conf/ols+ks2005/linus-fun-sm.jpg)](<https://lwn.net/Articles/144276/>)

  * [The hardware vendors' panel](<https://lwn.net/Articles/144269/>), on the impedance mismatch between the kernel development community and manufacturers. 

  * [Report from the networking summit](<https://lwn.net/Articles/144272/>) which was held before the kernel event. 

  * [The convergence of storage and network paths](<https://lwn.net/Articles/144273/>); how do you ensure safe operation when distinction between the networking and block subsystems blurs? 

  * [Clustering](<https://lwn.net/Articles/144274/>): a brief report from the clustering summit held two weeks before in Germany. 

  * [RAS tools](<https://lwn.net/Articles/144277/>), being mostly a discussion of the recently merged kexec and kdump capabilities. 

  * [Realtime capabilities](<https://lwn.net/Articles/144278/>), a look at the various proposals for implementing realtime response with Linux. 

  * [The kernel and the Linux desktop](<https://lwn.net/Articles/144279/>); a report from the Desktop Developers' Conference. 

  * [A report from the power management summit](<https://lwn.net/Articles/144193/>), contributed by Pat Mochel. Pat also led the session at the Kernel Summit on power management. The one thing that session added which is not in Pat's report: Linus took the power management developers to task for focusing on suspend-to-disk capabilities, when, he says, what everybody wants is suspend-to-RAM. The latter is complicated, however, by the usual video adapter difficulties. 

  * [The kernel development process](<https://lwn.net/Articles/144281/>), with an emphasis on how the community could produce kernels with fewer bugs.
